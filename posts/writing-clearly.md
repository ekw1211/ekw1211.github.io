A colleague once returned a draft to me with a single note in the margin: *Who is this for?*

It stopped me cold. I had been writing for myself — for the pleasure of precision, for the satisfaction of completeness — and had forgotten that a paper is a communication, not a monument.

## The reader's contract

When someone opens your paper, they have made an implicit trade: their attention in exchange for something useful. Failing to honor that contract isn't just an aesthetic failing. It's a failure of academic ethics.

Clear writing begins with a clear understanding of what you are claiming and why it matters. If the introduction is hard to write, it often means the contribution isn't yet understood, even by the author.

## Practical suggestions

- Write the abstract last, when you actually know what the paper argues.
- Read the introduction aloud. If you stumble, the sentence needs work.
- Every paragraph should have a job. If you can't say what job a paragraph does, cut it.

Clarity is not simplification. It is the removal of everything that stands between the idea and the reader.
